# Apex-Centaur — Algorithmic Fairness and Responsible AI Governance

**AI Ethics | Algorithmic Bias | Fairness Auditing | Privacy | Human Oversight**

**Master of Artificial Intelligence — Seneca Polytechnic**

## Project Overview

This individual portfolio analysis examines Apex-Centaur, a fictional AI-assisted leadership evaluation system, to investigate fairness, workplace surveillance, and accountability in automated promotion decisions.

The analysis builds on an academic case study and explores how unreliable measures of leadership and historical language biases can contribute to unequal outcomes.

## Key Ethical Challenges

- **Construct validity:** Assertive Slack messages and uninterrupted computer activity do not reliably measure leadership ability.
- **Embedding bias:** A hypothetical Word2Vec example illustrates how historical occupational stereotypes may be reflected in AI representations.
- **Workplace surveillance:** Monitoring keyboard activity and screen time may undervalue mentoring and collaboration.
- **Privacy:** Facial recognition and audio-sentiment monitoring create additional privacy and proportionality concerns.
- **Accountability:** Automated employment decisions require meaningful human review and appropriate safeguards.

## Fairness Audit

The fictional case dataset contains 200 audited promotion decisions.

| Metric | Men | Women |
|---|---:|---:|
| Selection rate | 55% | 20% |
| False-negative rate | 10% | 62.5% |
| False-positive rate | 20% | 8.33% |

**Disparate Impact Ratio:** 0.364

The observed disparities indicate potential fairness concerns but do not, on their own, prove discriminatory intent or a legal violation.

## Proposed Solutions

1. **Threshold tuning:** Evaluate alternative decision thresholds using selection rates, false-negative rates, and false-positive rates.
2. **Human oversight:** Require trained reviewers, accountable decision-makers, and independent appeals.
3. **Privacy protection:** Use proportionate mentoring records instead of intrusive biometric or audio surveillance.
4. **Governance:** Monitor fairness metrics, document decisions, and establish escalation and suspension criteria.

These are proposed mitigations, not experimentally validated improvements.
